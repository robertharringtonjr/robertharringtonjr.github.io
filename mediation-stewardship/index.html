Supporting Peace Through Conflict Literacy

The Mediation Stewardship Project was developed in support of the broader vision of a more peaceful Baltimore. It seeks to contribute practical communication and conflict-resolution education to community peacebuilding efforts, including the Baltimore Peace Movement.

The Mediation Stewardship Project is an independent Harrington Mediation initiative created to support community peace, constructive communication, and collaborative conflict engagement.

Our Mission

The Mediation Stewardship Project exists to help individuals, families, schools, businesses, and communities recognize mediation as an everyday life skill rather than only a legal or court-based service.

The project advances public conflict literacy by translating mediation principles into practical skills people can use in homes, classrooms, workplaces, neighborhoods, organizations, and ordinary conversations.

The objective is not to train everyone to become a professional mediator. The objective is to help more people listen carefully, communicate constructively, recognize competing interests, navigate disagreement, and respond to conflict without unnecessarily damaging relationships.

Why Mediation Stewardship Matters

The Hidden Mediator

People negotiate constantly—with themselves, their children, families, coworkers, neighbors, and communities. Many people already use mediation-informed skills without recognizing them.

Mediation Has an Image Problem

The word mediation often evokes courts, attorneys, divorce, paperwork, and formal disputes. The project translates mediation into language people already understand: listening, working things out, hearing both sides, and finding common ground.

Conflict Is Not Failure

Conflict reflects different experiences, needs, priorities, fears, and values. The central question is not whether conflict can be eliminated, but whether people can navigate it without destroying relationships.

Stewardship, Not Recruitment

The project is not built around recruiting more mediators. It is built around cultivating communities that naturally value listening, communication, collaboration, and mediation.

Workshops & Public Presentations

Mediation Stewardship workshops are educational experiences, not mediator-certification courses. Each workshop is designed to translate mediation-informed skills into practical tools participants can apply immediately.

  • Active Listening in Everyday Life
  • Preparing for Difficult Conversations
  • Assertive Versus Aggressive Communication
  • Recognizing Interests Beneath Positions
  • Healthy Disagreement
  • Perspective-Taking and Reflection
  • Family and Co-Parenting Communication
  • Workplace and Leadership Communication
  • Neighborhood Conflict
  • Conflict Literacy for Young Adults
Workshop Access and Pricing: Community workshops may be offered free to participants through partnerships, grants, or sponsorships. Private workshops for schools, businesses, churches, nonprofits, professional associations, and other organizations are developed and priced according to scope, audience, duration, and customization.

Youth Conflict Literacy Initiative

Young people are routinely taught mathematics, science, history, and language. Far fewer are intentionally taught how to disagree, listen, apologize, negotiate, regulate emotions, or communicate across differences.

The Youth Conflict Literacy Initiative will offer age-appropriate programming for middle schools, high schools, colleges, youth organizations, and leadership programs. Participants will not be trained as professional mediators. They will practice the foundational skills needed to navigate conflict competently throughout life.

Middle Schools

Listening, emotional awareness, apology, peer conflict, and respectful disagreement.

High Schools

Communication styles, difficult conversations, leadership, online conflict, and negotiation.

Colleges

Conflict literacy, collaboration, workplace preparation, campus dialogue, and emerging leadership.

Conflict Support Line

In Development

The project envisions a Conflict Support Line for people who need immediate guidance preparing for a difficult conversation, understanding a conflict, or identifying an appropriate next step.

The initial model may include scheduled callbacks, brief conflict coaching, resource navigation, and referrals to mediation or other appropriate services. It will not function as emergency response, crisis intervention, legal advice, or mental-health treatment.

Operational standards, availability, referral procedures, training requirements, safety protocols, and sponsorship support will be established before the line launches publicly.
