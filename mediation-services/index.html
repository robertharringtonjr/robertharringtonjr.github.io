Who May Benefit From Mediation?

Mediation may be appropriate for individuals, families, organizations, and communities seeking a structured conversation around conflict, communication, decision-making, or unresolved concerns.

  • Family and co-parenting conflicts
  • Workplace disagreements
  • Community disputes
  • Interpersonal conflicts
  • Organizational disagreements
  • Professional collaboration concerns

When Mediation May Not Be Appropriate

Mediation is voluntary and may not be suitable in every circumstance. Situations involving immediate safety concerns, coercion, unwillingness to participate, or the need for additional legal, therapeutic, or protective support may require alternative approaches.

Each inquiry is reviewed individually. The purpose of the inquiry process is to determine whether mediation may be appropriate and what next steps may be helpful.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is mediation?

Mediation is a voluntary process in which a neutral third party facilitates constructive conversation regarding conflict, disagreement, or important decision-making.

The mediator does not decide outcomes, determine fault, or impose solutions. Participants remain responsible for their own decisions.

Who may benefit from mediation?

Individuals, families, organizations, and communities may benefit from mediation when they are seeking a structured environment for addressing conflict, communication challenges, or unresolved concerns.

Is mediation confidential?

Yes. Mediation is generally confidential. Confidentiality primarily applies from the mediator to the participants.

Exceptions may exist where disclosure is required by law or necessary to address serious safety concerns.

Do I need an attorney?

No. Participants do not need an attorney to participate in mediation.

Attorneys are welcome at any stage of the process, and participants may seek independent legal advice whenever they choose.

What happens during mediation?

Participants have an opportunity to discuss concerns, perspectives, misunderstandings, and goals while the mediator facilitates constructive dialogue.

The process is designed to support communication, understanding, and informed decision-making.

How long does mediation take?

The length of mediation depends upon the complexity of the matter, the number of participants involved, and the goals of the participants.

What types of conflicts can be mediated?

Family disputes, co-parenting concerns, workplace disagreements, organizational conflicts, community disputes, interpersonal disagreements, and professional collaboration concerns may all be appropriate for mediation.

Mediation Philosophy

Understanding Precedes Resolution

Conflict often develops in environments where people feel unheard, misunderstood, dismissed, or uncertain.

My approach to mediation is grounded in the belief that meaningful understanding often creates opportunities for movement that persuasion alone cannot achieve.

While agreements may occur, the deeper objective is to create a space where participants can engage conflict with greater clarity, empowerment, recognition, and respect.

Fees & Scheduling

Harrington Mediation LLC believes that conflict-resolution services should be professional, accessible, and transparent. The following information is provided to help participants understand the general fee structure and scheduling process for mediation services.

Free 15-Minute Consultation

Prospective participants are invited to schedule a complimentary 15-minute consultation to discuss their situation, ask questions about the mediation process, and determine whether mediation may be appropriate for their needs.

Standard Mediation Session

Up to 2 Hours — $250

This fee includes:

  • Session preparation
  • Administrative review
  • Mediation services
  • Basic post-session administrative processing

Additional Time

$100 per additional hour

Additional time is subject to participant agreement and mediator availability.

Scheduling Deposit

A $50 scheduling deposit is required to reserve a mediation date.

The deposit is applied toward the total mediation fee and helps secure dedicated time on the mediation calendar.

Sliding Scale Opportunities

Limited reduced-fee services may be available for:

  • Veterans
  • Students
  • Community referrals
  • Select pro bono matters

Availability is limited and determined on a case-by-case basis.

Scheduling

Mediation sessions are generally scheduled one session at a time based upon participant readiness, mediator availability, and the needs of the matter.

Accessibility, Inclusion & Respect

Every person experiences and communicates with the world in unique ways. Differences in culture, background, language, disability, neurodivergence, life experience, communication style, and personal identity may influence how individuals participate in mediation.

Harrington Mediation strives to create an environment where participants feel respected, heard, and able to participate meaningfully in the process. Mediation is most effective when people are given the space to communicate in ways that support their understanding, expression, and self-determination.

If accommodations or adjustments would support your participation, those needs are welcome and may be discussed before or during the mediation process.

Understanding does not require sameness. Meaningful dialogue often begins when we recognize that people may experience the same situation in very different ways.
